Cleansing & Charging Guide

Crystals are traditionally cleansed to clear absorbed energy, then charged to restore or boost their own. Not every method suits every stone — soft or water-soluble crystals in particular need gentler care. Check the safety table below before soaking or sunning a stone you're unsure about.

Moonlight

Leave crystals on a windowsill or outdoors overnight, ideally under a full moon. Gentle and safe for virtually every stone, including soft or water-sensitive ones.

Sunlight

A few hours of morning sun can cleanse and energize many crystals.

Caution: prolonged direct sun fades purple, pink, and yellow stones (amethyst, rose quartz, citrine, fluorite, celestite, and others) — check the table below.

Running Water

Holding a crystal under a natural stream or tap water is a classic cleansing method for durable stones.

Avoid for soft or soluble stones: selenite, angelite, celestite, howlite, lepidolite, calcite, turquoise, and copper-based stones like malachite and azurite (which can also be toxic when wet). See the table below.

Smoke (Smudging)

Pass the crystal through the smoke of sage, palo santo, or incense. Safe for every stone since there's no direct contact with water or sun.

Sound

A singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork played near the crystal is considered a safe cleansing method for any stone, regardless of hardness or solubility.

Earth / Salt Burial

Burying a durable crystal in soil or dry salt overnight is a traditional grounding cleanse.

Avoid salt (and often direct soil contact) for soft, porous, or layered stones — salt can scratch or corrode them.

Crystal Cluster or Selenite

Place a crystal on a clear quartz cluster or selenite slab/bowl overnight. Both are considered self-cleansing and safe for resting almost any other stone on top.

Visualization & Intention

Hold the crystal and visualize white or golden light clearing it, then set a fresh intention. Works for every stone and pairs well with any other method above.

Per-Crystal Safety Reference

"Caution" means brief contact is generally fine but prolonged exposure can damage or fade the stone; "Avoid" means skip that method entirely.
